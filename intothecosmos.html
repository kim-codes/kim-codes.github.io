Behind the Work

Making a
Complex Product
Memorable

How do you make a technical product memorable without making it feel less technical?

Sometimes the answer isn't simplifying the technology. It's finding a story people want to follow.

Mission 01

The Mission

Building field confidence for a complex cloud database product.

Create a scalable technical enablement program that helped sellers and technical teams confidently position a distrubuted database product in customer conversations.

Mission 02

The Challenge

How do you make one product memorable inside an organization with hundreds of competing priorities?

The challenge wasn't that the product lacked capability. It was that sellers and field teams needed to confidently explain the value in a way customers could understand, identify the right customer scenarios, and translate complex technical concepts into customer conversations.

They didn't need to become distributed database experts overnight per se. They needed a lightweight way to understand when and why to position it and how to communicate its value with confidence.

Mission 03

The Blueprint

People remember stories before they remember features.

Building another training deck wasn't going to solve the problem.

There was simply too much information. Sellers didn't need to know everything; they needed to know the few things that would help them have more meaningful customer conversations. I focused on the customer problems it solved, the scenarios where it was the right fit, and the stories that made those use cases easy to remember.

One thing I'd learned was that sellers listen to each other.

We reinforced these ideas through peer-led stories. Repeatable resources. And practical examples from the field.
The goal wasn't to teach every feature. It was to help people feel confident enough to start the conversation.

Mission 04

The Launch

Into the Cosmos: A Coding Odyssey.

Due to the product name the space theme felt like a natural way to enhance the learning experience and give people something they could immediately connect with. Inspired by Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey, I built Into the Cosmos around the idea that technical enablement could be both fun and valuable.

Rather than treating learning as a one-time event, it became an ongoing experience that combined storytelling, technical education, community, and practical resources to help the field build confidence over time.

I knew people were tired of dry enablement, and it felt like a risk worth taking. The goal wasn't to make the technology simpler. It was to make people excited enough to explore it.t.

Mission 05

The Legacy

✓ The audience grew. Recurring cadence created anticipation, similar to a show. People were asking when the next episode was coming.

✓ People wanted to contribute. Teams asked for follow-up content and deeper technical episodes. Others wanted to participate in epiosodes.

✓ Champions grew organically.

Mission 06

Mission Debrief

Lessons from building confidence.

Key takeaway

One thing I learned is that people don't adopt products because they've completed training.
They adopt products because they feel confident talking about them.

What I underestimated

This project changed how I think about enablement.
I assumed if we built great technical content, people would naturally find it.
They didn't.
I had to create moments, community, and repetition before the content itself started to matter.

The goal isn't to create more content. It's to change behavior. Content is just one of the tools.

Enablement is rarely a content issue.
It's a behavior change problem.
